# Neanderthals and other Homo

Several other human populations lived alongside Homo sapiens during the Late Pleistocene (129000-11700 ya). Neanderthals occupied much of Europe and western Asia, while Denisovans inhabited parts of Asia. They were capable hunters, made sophisticated tools and showed evidence of social and symbolic behavior. Some also interbred with Homo sapiens, leaving traces of their DNA in modern populations.

## Timeline

### Neanderthals

- ~500–400 thousand BCE: Diverge from the lineage leading to modern humans, probably in Eurasia.
- ~400–40 thousand BCE: Inhabit much of Europe and western Asia; adapted to cold and highly seasonal environments.
- ~250–40 thousand BCE: Hunt large animals, use sophisticated stone tools, control fire and probably use clothing and shelters.
- ~100–40 thousand BCE: Bury some of their dead and show evidence of caring.
- ~70–40 thousand BCE: Use pigments, personal ornaments and possibly symbolic objects (debated).
- ~60–40 thousand BCE: Coexist with Homo sapiens in parts of Eurasia and interbreed (non-African populations inherit ~1–2% DNA).
- ~40 thousand BCE: Disappear possibly due to low densities, isolation, inbreeding, environmental changes and competition.

### Denisovans

- ~400–200 thousand BCE: Diverge from the Neanderthal lineage and spread across parts of Asia.
- ~200–50 thousand BCE: Inhabit regions extending from Siberia into eastern and southeastern Asia.
- ~100–50 thousand BCE: Interbreed with Homo sapiens (people in Oceania and Southeast Asia inherit their DNA).
- ~50 thousand BCE: Ancestry contributes to adaptations in modern populations (high-altitude survival in Tibetans).
- ~50 thousand BCE: DNA is high in some Melanesian and Aboriginal Australian populations.

### Homo floresiensis

- ~100–50 thousand BCE: Lives on Flores, Indonesia; its small body and brain evolved in an isolated island environment.
- ~100–50 thousand BCE: Makes and uses stone tools despite its very small brain.
- ~50 thousand BCE: Disappears from Flores (Homo Sapiens?).
