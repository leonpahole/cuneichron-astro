# Carthage

Carthaginian wealth was based on Mediterranean trade and a network of Phoenician-founded settlements. Its position near routes between Africa, Sicily, and the western Mediterranean made it a major commercial hub.

It was governed as an oligarchic republic dominated by wealthy families, with annually elected Suffets as chief executives. The military forces relied heavily on hired foreign troops, including Numidian cavalry, Spanish infantry, and Balearic slingers.

The term “Punic,” derived from the Latin word for Phoenician, was used by Romans to describe the Carthaginians.

## Timeline

- ~800 BCE: Founded by Phoenician settlers.
- ~600–500 BCE: Influence expands across western Mediterranean, with trading posts in North Africa, Iberia, Sardinia, Corsica, and Sicily.
- ~550 BCE: Phoenician settlements increasingly under Carthaginian influence.
- ~480 BCE: Carthage defeated by Greeks at Himera (Sicily), limiting their expansion on the island.
- ~406–396 BCE: Carthage captures several Greek cities in Sicily but not Syracuse.
- 315–300 BCE: Carthage fought Syracuse (king Agathocles) for control of Sicily, ending in stalemate and Carthage holding west.
- 264–241 BCE: First Punic war.
- 241–238 BCE: Carthage faced a major revolt by unpaid mercenaries after the war, eventually defeating them.
