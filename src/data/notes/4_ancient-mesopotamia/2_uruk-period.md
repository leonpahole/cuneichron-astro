# The Uruk period

Emergence of first true cities and increasingly complex systems of administration, religion and trade. Temples were major economic and religious institutions, while agricultural surplus supported specialized labor and growing social hierarchies. Uruk became the largest and most influential urban center, spreading its cultural practices across much of Mesopotamia and beyond.

## Timeline

- ~4500–3000 BCE: Eridu, Uruk and other settlements grow into complex urban centers.
- ~3500–3300 BCE: Monumental temple complexes, including the Eanna and Anu precincts, dominate Uruk's urban landscape (Inanna was patron deity).
- ~3500–3200 BCE: Proto-cuneiform develops, initially used for administrative records.
- ~3500–3000 BCE: Cylinder seals become widespread for administration, trade and identification.
- ~3500–3000 BCE: Wheeled vehicles and the potter's wheel emerge, among the earliest known uses of the wheel.
- ~3300–3000 BCE: Temples become major religious, economic and administrative institutions, collecting agricultural surplus and redistributing goods to workers and specialists. Religious, economic and administrative authority are closely intertwined, although the extent to which temples directly controlled the entire economy remains uncertain.
- ~3500–3000 BCE: Specialized labor expands, including craftspeople, textile workers, metalworkers, administrators and priests.
- ~3500–3000 BCE: Long-distance trade flourishes; lapis lazuli reaches Mesopotamia from Badakhshan in present-day Afghanistan.
- ~3500–3000 BCE: Early glass-like materials such as faience and frit are produced in Mesopotamia and Egypt. These are glazed materials rather than true glass.
- ~3300–3100 BCE: Proto-cuneiform becomes increasingly standardized, accompanied by early lexical lists used in scribal education.
- ~3300–3100 BCE: Uruk material culture and administrative practices spread widely across Mesopotamia and neighbouring regions through trade, migration and possibly colonies.
- ~3200–3100 BCE: _Uruk's urban_ population and monumental architecture reach their peak.
