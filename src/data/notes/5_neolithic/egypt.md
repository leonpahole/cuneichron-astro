# Egypt

Communities developed in a changing Saharan environment, initially relying on seasonal lakes, hunting, herding and gathering. As the Sahara became increasingly arid, populations concentrated along the Nile, where farming and permanent settlement became possible. Increasing wealth, trade and craft specialization contributed to social hierarchy and the emergence of regional centers.

## Timeline

- ~9000–5000 BCE: Semi-nomadic cattle-herding communities occupy the eastern Sahara during a wetter climatic period.
- ~9000–5000 BCE: Cattle appear to have important symbolic and ritual significance, reflected in cattle burials and ceremonial sites.
- ~6000–5000 BCE: Seasonal lakes and grasslands support cattle herding, hunting and gathering across areas that are now desert.
- ~6000–5000 BCE: Ritual practices develop around cattle, including deliberate cattle burials and monumental stone structures.
- ~6000–5000 BCE: Nabta Playa becomes an important ritual center, with stone structures and evidence of cattle-related ceremonies.
- ~5000–4000 BCE: Increasing aridity transforms the Sahara into desert, encouraging populations to concentrate along the Nile Valley.
- ~5000–4000 BCE: Permanent agricultural settlements develop along the Nile, cultivating crops and keeping domesticated animals.
- ~5000–4000 BCE: Regional religious traditions develop around particular animals, natural forces and local sacred sites.
- ~4500–4000 BCE: Badarian culture develops in Upper Egypt, characterized by settled agriculture, distinctive pottery, personal ornaments and increasingly differentiated burials.
- ~4500–4000 BCE: Increasing differences in grave goods indicate growing social inequality and emerging elites.
- ~4500–4000 BCE: Trade networks connect the Nile Valley with the Eastern Desert, Red Sea and regions farther south and east.
