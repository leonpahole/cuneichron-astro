# Early Roman republic

Early Republican Rome was an oligarchy dominated by patricians (descendans of the original senators), while plebeians had limited political power and pressed for greater equality. Each consul had near-kingly executive power, but could veto the other and had to surrender power after one year. Dictator was a temporary emergency magistrate granted near-absolute authority, normally for a maximum of six months, to deal with a crisis before relinquishing power.

Most Romans were farmers, with land concentrated among wealthy elites; tensions over debt, land, and political representation drove the Conflict of the Orders. Women, slaves, and immigrants had virtually no political rights. Religion remained important, particularly through sacred oaths. External wars were particularly against the Volscians, Aequi, Veii, and Sabines.

## Timeline

### Founding of the Republic

- 509 BCE: Brutus and Collatinus became first consuls, swearing to prevent the return of monarchy; Collatinus soon resigned due to his ties to the Tarquins and was replaced by Publicola.
- 509 BCE: Brutus executed his own sons for participating in a conspiracy to restore the monarchy.
- 509 BCE: Rome defeated an army from Veii attempting to restore Tarquin; Brutus was killed in battle.
- ~509 BCE: Publicola introduced the right to appeal magistrates' decisions and made it legal to kill anyone attempting to make himself king.

### Early wars

- ~508 BCE: Lars Porsena of Clusium may have captured Rome, possibly to restore the Tarquins; Roman tradition portrayed it as a Roman victory.
- ~496 BCE: Rome defeated a Latin coalition at Lake Regillus amid struggles for regional dominance and alleged attempts to restore the Tarquins.
- ~480s BCE: Rome fought recurring wars against the Volsci and Aequi over farmland, settlements, and control of Latium's southern and eastern frontiers.
- 460 BCE: A Sabine led an uprising of exiles and enslaved people, briefly seizing Rome's Capitoline Hill in an attempt to overthrow the government.
- 458 BCE: Cincinnatus became dictator to rescue a Roman army trapped by the Aequi, defeating them before relinquishing power.

### Conflict of the Orders

- 494 BCE: Plebeians seceded over debt and political grievances, leading to the creation of sacrosanct tribunes to defend their interests.
- 471 BCE: Tribunes were now elected from the Tribal rather than Centuriate Assembly, reducing patrician influence.
- 460 BCE: Cincinnatus served as consul and opposed plebeian reforms; his career later became a symbol of Roman republican virtue.
- 451–449 BCE: The decemvirs produced the Twelve Tables, Rome's earliest written legal code (legal procedure, debt, inheritance, marriage, property, criminal penalties); their refusal to relinquish power led to popular resistance and restoration of regular magistracies.
- 445–444 BCE: Patrician–plebeian marriage was legalized; military tribunes with consular power was introduced, an office eventually accessible to plebeians.
- ~443 BCE: Censor was created to administer the census (later morality and senate membership); it became an important patrician-controlled magistracy.
- 439 BCE: During a grain shortage, Malius distributed free grain and was accused of seeking kingship and killed on the orders of the dictator Cincinnatus.

### The war with Veii

- ~435–425 BCE: Rome fought to retain control of Fidenae, an important settlement on the Tiber, amid renewed conflict with Veii.
- ~425 BCE: Rome recaptured Fidenae, strengthening its position.
- ~400 BCE: Rome began a major war against Veii, driven by competition over trade, salt, and fertile land.
- ~400–396 BCE: Soldier pay was introduced to compensate troops kept away from their farms as siege of Veii required year-round army.
- ~396 BCE: Camillus was appointed dictator and finally captured and sacked Veii; Romans settled it, taking advantage of fertile soil to end the famine.
- ~395–394 BCE: Camillus went into voluntary exile after political conflict over his handling of Veii's spoils.

### The Gallic Sack and recovery

- ~390 BCE: The Senones, a Gallic tribe traditionally led by Brennus, defeated Rome at the Allia River, occupied and sacked the city, while Roman defenders held the Capitoline Hill. According to tradition, Rome paid a ransom for their withdrawal.
- ~390–380s BCE: Rome rebuilt rather than relocate to Veii, despite fire damage and threats from neighbours. Reconstruction contributed to the city's irregular layout.
- ~380s BCE: Camillus repeatedly commanded Roman armies during Rome's recovery, fighting neighboring enemies including the Volsci and Aequi.
- ~380s–370s BCE: Debt, unequal access to public land, and exclusion from high office intensified plebeian demands for reform.
- 367–366 BCE: The Licinio-Sextian reforms opened consulship to plebeians, addressed debt (interest and time) and restrictions on landholding.
- ~365 BCE: Death of Camillus marked the end of an era of Roman recovery after the sack.
