# The year of the four emperors

Nero's death ended the Julio-Claudian dynasty without an established succession mechanism. Rival provincial commanders competed for imperial power, demonstrating that control of the legions, rather than dynastic inheritance alone, could determine who became emperor.

## Vespasian - early career

- ~43–47 CE: Commanded a legion during the Roman conquest of Britain, earning triumphal honors for his victories.
- 51 CE: Served as consul, establishing himself as a senior political and military figure.
- ~63–64 CE: Governed the province of Africa, reportedly avoiding the widespread personal enrichment.
- 66 CE: Despite previously falling out of Nero's favor, Vespasian was appointed commander of Roman forces against the Jewish revolt.

## Galba - early career

An elderly aristocrat and experienced provincial governor known for strict military discipline, conservative values, and frugality. His refusal to reward soldiers and dependence on powerful advisers undermined his short reign.

- 20-42 CE: Served as praetor, consul and commanded Upper German army, establishing a reputation for strict military discipline.
- 61–68 CE: Galba governed Hispania Tarraconensis.
- 68 CE: Galba relied heavily on three influential advisers: Titus Vinius, Cornelius Laco, and the freedman Icelus.

## Otho - early career

A former companion of Nero who became an unexpectedly capable provincial governor after falling out of imperial favor. Ambitious for the throne, he supported Galba while hoping to become his adopted successor.

- ~58–59 CE: Nero appointed Otho governor of Lusitania following a dispute involving Poppaea Sabina, Otho's wife and Nero's future wife.
- ~58–68 CE: Governed Lusitania competently despite his earlier reputation for extravagance and political inexperience.
- 68 CE: Supported Galba's challenge to Nero and sought to secure his position as Galba's eventual successor.

## Vitellius - early career

An aristocrat and former provincial governor known for his sociability, extravagant lifestyle, and popularity among soldiers. His relaxed leadership contrasted sharply with Galba's strict discipline, helping him gain the support of the Rhine legions.

- 48-61 CE: Served as consul and governor of Africa.

## Galba ascends to the throne

- June–July 68 CE: Galba consolidated his authority in Hispania, raised a new legion, and appointed a new Spanish governor.
- ~June–October 68 CE: During his journey to Rome he ordered harsh reprisals against opponents, damaging his reputation.
- ~68 CE: Otho supported Galba's accession and sought adoption as his heir, gaining influence through gifts and promises to Preatorian guard.
- ~October 68 CE: Arrived in Rome, violently suppressed a demonstration by former sailors seeking promotion to legionaires, and undermined his popularity due to his harsh treatment of opponents and strict discipline.
- ~68 CE: Attempted to restore imperial finances by recovering gifts distributed under Nero, provoking resentment.
- ~68 CE: Refused to pay the Praetorian Guard the bribe promised by Sabinus. Sabinus attempted to become emperor but was killed by his soldiers.
- 10 January 69 CE: Adopted Piso as heir, rejecting Otho despite his expectations.
- 15 January 69 CE: Galba and Piso were assassinated by Praetorian soldiers supporting Otho.

## Otho ascends to the throne

- 2 January 69 CE: Rhine legions proclaimed Vitellius emperor after refusing allegiance to Galba.
- 15 January 69 CE: Otho became emperor but faced an immediate challenge from Vitellius.
- ~January–March 69 CE: Failed negotiations with Vitellius led to civil war. Otho mobilized the Praetorian Guard and summoned Balkan reinforcements.
- March 69 CE: Vitellius's armies invaded northern Italy under Valens and Caecina, but Otho's forces successfully defended Placentia.
- 14 April 69 CE: Otho's army was defeated at the First Battle of Bedriacum, before all his reinforcements had arrived.
- 16 April 69 CE: Otho committed suicide to avoid prolonging the civil war.

## Vitellius ascends to the throne

- April 69 CE: Following Otho's death, Vitellius became the uncontested emperor.
- ~April–July 69 CE: Vitellius travelled to Rome, with his undisciplined army causing considerable damage along the way.
- ~69 CE: Vitellius left much of the administration to his generals Valens and Caecina, whose rivalry undermined the stability of his government.
- ~69 CE: Replaced the Praetorian Guard with soldiers from the Rhine legions, weakening his field armies and driving former Praetorians towards Vespasian.

## Vespasian's rebellion

- ~June 68–June 69 CE: Vespasian suspended operations against the Jewish revolt and remained neutral during the struggle between Galba, Otho and Vitellius.
- 1 July 69 CE: Prefect of Egypt proclaimed Vespasian emperor, followed shortly afterwards by the legions of Judaea, Syria and the Balkans.
- ~July–August 69 CE: Vespasian secured Egypt and its vital grain supply, while the governor of Syria, Mucianus, prepared an invasion of Italy.
- ~August–October 69 CE: Antonius Primus led the Danubian legions into Italy without waiting for Mucianus, while Caecina unsuccessfully attempted to defect and was imprisoned by his own troops.
- 24–25 October 69 CE: Flavian forces defeated the Vitellian army at the Second Battle of Bedriacum and sacked Cremona.
- ~November–December 69 CE: Provincial governors increasingly defected to Vespasian, while Valens was captured and executed.
- 18–19 December 69 CE: Vitellius attempted to abdicate but was prevented by his supporters. Fighting erupted in Rome, resulting in the destruction of the Temple of Jupiter on the Capitoline Hill and the execution of Vespasian's brother Sabinus.
- 20 December 69 CE: Flavian forces captured Rome and killed Vitellius.
- 21 December 69 CE: The Senate recognized Vespasian as emperor.
