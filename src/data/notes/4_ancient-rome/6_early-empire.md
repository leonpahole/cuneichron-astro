# Early Roman Empire

## Timeline

### Octavian consolidates power

- 29 BCE: Octavian celebrated three triumphs for his victories in Illyricum, Actium, and Egypt.
- ~28 BCE: Octavian and Agrippa reduced civil-warm armies, stationing some in frontiers and conducated a census to review Senate membership.
- ~28–25 BCE: Agrippa constructed the original Pantheon, while Augustus dedicated the Temple of Apollo on the Palatine Hill.
- 27 BCE: Octavian restored Senate authority but received 10 years of provincial control with most Roman legions and honorific title Augustus. He called himself princeps (first citizen), maintaining republican traditions while having authority.
- ~29–19 BCE: Virgil composed the Aeneid, connecting Rome's mythical origins and the Julian family to Aeneas, helping legitimize Augustus's rule.

### Reforms and succession crisis

- 27–19 BCE: Augustus pursued the conquest of northern Hispania (Cantabrian Wars).
- 25 BCE: Augustus arranged the marriage of his daughter Julia to his nephew Marcellus, whom he favored as a successor.
- 23 BCE: Augustus became ill, exposing the uncertainty of succession.
- 23 BCE: Augustus resigned consulship and received perpetual tribuneship + proconsular authority, allowing him control without monopolizing the consulship.
- 23 BCE: Marcellus died unexpectedly, disrupting Augustus's succession plans and increasing Agrippa's importance.
- 22 BCE: During a severe grain shortage, populace called for Augustus to become dictator but he refused, preserving the republican appearance.
- 21 BCE: Arranged the marriage of Julia to Marcus Agrippa, strengthening the succession plans.
- 20 BCE: Diplomatic agreement with Parthia, recovering Roman legionary standards.
- ~19–18 BCE: Reorganized the Senate (decision body) and established permanent provinical administrative staffs.
- ~20 BCE: Established more roads and relay stations to improve communication across the empire.
- 18 BCE: Introduced legislation encouraging marriage and childbearing, while imposing restrictions on unmarried and childless and banning adultery.
- 17 BCE: Adopted his grandsons Gaius and Lucius Caesar, intending to prepare them as future successors.
- 16 BCE: Augustus decided a good northern border would be on Danube and Elbe.
- ~16–15 BCE: Rome consolidated control over Noricum and Raetia, in preparation for extending the northern frontier toward the Danube.
- 12 BCE: Marcus Agrippa died, creating uncertainty over the imperial succession. Augustus arranged for Tiberius to divorce Vipsania and marry Julia.
- 12 BCE: Augustus became Pontifex Maximus following the death of Lepidus in 13 BCE, consolidating his religious authority.
- ~12–9 BCE: Tiberius led campaigns in Pannonia, extending Roman control toward the Danube.
- 12–9 BCE: Drusus led campaigns into Germania, reaching the Elbe River but failing to establish permanent control.
- 9 BCE: Drusus died following an accident during his Germanic campaign, leaving Tiberius as most likely potential successor.
- 8 BCE: Maecenas, Augustus's longtime political adviser and patron of Roman literature, died.
- 6 BCE: Tiberius unexpectedly withdrew from public life to Rhodes, possibly because of his unhappy marriage and uncertain succession.
- 2 BCE: Augustus exiled his daughter Julia following accusations of adultery.
- 1 BCE–4 CE: Gaius Caesar led a diplomatic and military mission in the East to secure Roman influence in Armenia and negotiate with Parthia.
- 2 CE: Lucius Caesar died unexpectedly from illness while traveling to Hispania.
- 4 CE: Gaius Caesar died following complications from a wound sustained during a campaign in Armenia.

### Transfer of power

- 4 CE: Augustus adopted Tiberius as his heir, requiring him to adopt Germanicus.
- 7–8 CE: Augustus exiled his grandson Agrippa Postumus and granddaughter Julia the Younger, amid accusations of misconduct.
- ~7–9 CE: Rome expanded military presence east of the Rhine, establishing forts and alliances with Germanic communities.
- September 9 CE: Battle of the Teutoburg Forest: a coalition of Germanic warriors led by Arminius ambushed and destroyed three Roman legions.
- ~9–11 CE: Tiberius led campaigns to stabilize the Rhine frontier but Rome never established control over Germania east of the river.
- ~6 CE: Augustus established a treasury to finance veterans' retirement and reduce dependence on generals, and a permanent firefighting/night patrol service.
- ~1–14 CE: Augustus strengthened imperial administration, expanding the use of permanent officials and reforming provincial taxation to reduce corruption.
- ~9–14 CE: Following defeat in Germania, Augustus prioritized defending existing imperial territories rather than pursuing expansion.
- 14 CE: Augustus finalized an autobigraophy of his achievements, victories, spending, and honors.
- 19 August 14 CE: Augustus died at Nola, aged 75.
- August 14 CE: Agrippa Postumus, Augustus's exiled grandson and a potential rival to Tiberius, was executed shortly after Augustus's death.
- 14 CE: Tiberius succeeded Augustus as princeps. The Senate deified Augustus.

### Tiberius

- ~14–23 CE: Generally pursued a cautious foreign policy, avoiding major wars and emphasizing fiscal restraint and provincial administration.
- 14 CE: Legions in Pannonia and along the Rhine mutinied, demanding better benefits. Drusus the Younger (son of Tiberius) suppressed the Pannonian mutiny, Germanicus (nephew of Tiberius) supressed the Rhine one and rejected soldiers' suggestions that he challenge Tiberius.
- 15 CE: Lucius Aelius Sejanus became sole prefect of the Praetorian Guard.
- 15–16 CE: Germanicus led punitive campaigns across the Rhine against Germanic tribes, seeking revenge for Teutoburg Forest. He defeated forces led by Arminius several times but failed to establish lasting control over Germania.
- 17 CE: Out of fear of his popularity, Tiberius recalled Germanicus, threw him a triumph and appointed him authority over eastern provinces.
- 17–19 CE: Germanicus worked in eastern provinces to stabilize relations with client kingdoms, encountering opposition from Piso, governor of Syria.
- 19 CE: Germanicus visited Egypt without Tiberius's permission, violating restrictions on senatorial access.
- 10 October 19 CE: Germanicus died at Antioch following an illness. Suspicions of poisoning implicated Piso and, indirectly, Tiberius. Piso faced trial and died by suicide before the proceedings concluded, deepening public suspicion of Tiberius.
- ~15–23 CE: Sejanus became sole prefect of the Praetorian Guard, and concentrated them near Rome, strenghtening its political influence.
- 23 CE: Drusus died unexpectedly, possibly by poisoning from Sejanus and Drusus's wife Livilla.
- ~23–27 CE: Tiberius increasingly relied on Sejanus to manage affairs, while he eventually withdrew from Rome.
- 25-31 CE: Sejanus built power as Livia (mother of Tiberius) died, persecuting Agrippina and her sons and using accusations of treason to eliminate opponents and becoming consul. He was denied marrying Livilla, hoping to strengthen his position within the dynasty.
- 18 October 31 CE: Tiberius turned against Sejanus, arranging his arrest and execution. His downfall triggered prosecutions of his associates.
- 33 CE: Agrippina and her son died in captivity, eliminating two prominent members of Germanicus's family from the imperial succession.
