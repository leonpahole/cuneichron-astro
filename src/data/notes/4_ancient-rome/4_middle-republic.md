# Middle Roman republic

Rome emerged as the dominant power in central Italy. The political integration of plebeians (consulship + first plebeian censor and dictator) strengthened the Republic. Rome became involved in neighboring conflicts, with longer wars against stronger opponents and shifting from defense toward expansion (land, taxation, and manpower).

Phalanx was replaced with maniple. The infantry was divided into smaller maneuverable units arranged in three lines. It was combined with light infantry (for beginning skirmish) and cavalry (on sides). Allies had to provide forces, especially cavalry. Soldiers fought with thrown spears before closing with swords. Focus was on discipline (rewards and punishments), logistics, infrastrucutre (Appian way) and fortified camps. The legion consisted of 10 maniples of 120 young, 10 maniples of 120 experienced, and 10 half-strength maniples of veterans + 1200 light infantry and 300 cavalry (4500 in total).

At the end of this period, Rome’s overseas territories included Sicily, parts of Spain, Africa, and Greece, marking the emergence of a provincial empire. The conquests brought wealth, slaves, and political influence to the Republic, but the benefits were distributed unevenly.

## Timeline

### First Samnite war

- 343–341 BCE: Samnites (semi-nomads from central Appenines) came down from the hills and encroached on Campania; Rome got involved and won but without decisive victory. A post-war mutiny by Roman soldiers was peacefully resolved by Corvus.
- 342 BCE: Plebeian reforms: at least one consul is plebeian and ten-year period between same offices.

### Latin War

- 340-338 BCE: Latin League demanded full Roman citizenship and a Latin consul. With Samnite help, Rome defeated individual Latin communities, abolished Latin league, established colonies and granted forms of citizenship depending on conduct.

### Second Samnite War

- 327 BCE: Rome intervened in Naples, expelling its Samnite garrison.
- ~321 BCE: Samnites trapped and defeated a Roman army at Caudine Forks, leading to a five-year pause in fighting.
- 315 BCE: Rome suffered another major defeat at Lautulae, threatening its alliances.
- 312 BCE: Appius Claudius began construction of the Appian Way (Rome–Capua) to improve military transport and Rome's first aqueduct to supply fresh water.
- ~312 BCE: Phalanx replaced with the more flexible maniple system due to struggles in the wars.
- 311–308 BCE: Etruscans joined the war against Rome but were eventually defeated.
- 304 BCE: War ended in Roman victory, strengthening Rome's control over Samnium.
- ~304 BCE: Rome expanded its system of citizenship and military obligations among Italian communities.

### Third Samnite War

- 298-295 BCE: Samnites resisted further Roman settlement in Samnium; they joined forces with Etruscans, Umbrians and Gallic tribes from the Po valley.
- 295-293 BCE: Rome defeated the coalition at the Battle of Sentinum (295 BCE) and Samnites at Aquilonia (293 BCE).
- 290 BCE: The war ended, leaving Rome dominant across the Italian peninsula.

### Pyrrhic wars

- 282 BCE: Rome declared war on Tarentum (Magna Grecia) after a dispute with fleets.
- 280 BCE: Pyrrhus came to help Tarentum, invading Italy with an army including war elephants (Rome's first battle against them), defeating them at Heraclea.
- 279 BCE: Pyrrhus defeated Rome at the Battle of Asculum, but suffered heavy losses (“Pyrrhic victory”).
- 278-276 BCE: Pyrrhus left for Sicily to drive the Carthaginians off the island, achieving victories but failing to capture one remaining stronghold.
- 275 BCE: Pyrrhus returned to Italy and was defeated at Beneventum, withdrawing to Epirus.
- 272 BCE: Rome captured Tarentum, completing its conquest of Magna Graecia.

### First Punic war

- 289–264 BCE: The Mamertines seized Messana after Agathocles' death, later clashing with Syracuse (Hiero II). They sought protection from Carthage, then appealed to Rome.
- 264 BCE: War began after Rome intervened in Messana against Carthage and Syracuse. Hiero II subsequently became a Roman ally.
- 262 BCE: Rome captured Agrigentum, strengthening its position in Sicily.
- ~261 BCE: Rome built a large war fleet to challenge Carthaginian naval dominance, adapting Carthaginian designs.
- 260 BCE: After an initial naval defeat, Rome won its first major naval victory at Mylae using the `corvus`.
- ~260–256 BCE: The war settled into indecisive fighting on land and sea. Carthage harrassed Italian coast from Sardinia and Corsica.
- 256 BCE: Rome defeated the Carthaginian fleet at Ecnomus and invaded North Africa.
- 255 BCE: Carthage under Xanthippus defeated the Roman invasion at Tunis; a storm subsequently destroyed much of Rome's returning fleet.
- 249 BCE: Carthage defeated a Roman fleet at Drepana, while Hamilcar Barca later sustained resistance in western Sicily.
- 241 BCE: Rome won a decisive naval victory at the Aegates Islands, forcing Carthage to surrender Sicily and pay an indemnity.

### Between Punic wars

- 241 BCE: A Gallic tribe rebelled against Roman expansion in northern Italy but was defeated.
- 236 BCE: A coalition of Gallic tribes marched south against Rome but dissolved before engaging the Roman army.
- 228 BCE: Rome defeated the Illyrians after their piracy and established a protectorate in southern Illyria.
- 228 BCE: Rome established relations with Greeks and was accepted as a political equal, partly to counter growing Macedon.
- 226 BCE: A large Gallic coalition invaded northern Italy; Rome defeated them and cleared Gallic forces south of the Alps.

### Second Punic war

- 219–218 BCE: Hannibal's capture of Saguntum (Spain) triggered a diplomatic crisis, leading Rome to declare the Second Punic War.
- 218 BCE: Hannibal marched from Spain through Gaul and crossed the Alps into Italy, suffering heavy losses (50k -> 25k).
- 218 BCE: Hannibal defeated Roman forces at Ticinus and Trebia (tactics and superior Numidian cavalry), gaining support from Gallic tribes.
- 217 BCE: Hannibal crossed marshes of Etruria to bypass Roman-controlled roads, then ambushed and destroyed a Roman army at Lake Trasimene.
- 217 BCE: Dictator Fabius Maximus adopted a strategy of avoiding battle with Hannibal, facing strong opposition. Minucius was granted equal dictatorial authority, undermining the office's traditional supremacy, but was nearly defeated and rescued by Fabius.
- 216 BCE: Hannibal annihilated a much larger Roman army at Cannae using a double-envelopment maneuver.
- 216 BCE: Rome raised additional legions by changing age limits and entrolling slaves, while Hannibal encouraged Rome's Italian allies to defect.
- 216 BCE: Senate assumed greater control, sidelining popular assemblies and pursuing a more consistent military strategy.
- 216 BCE: Capua defected to Hannibal, becoming his most important Italian ally.
- 215–205 BCE: Philip V of Macedon allied with Hannibal, prompting Rome to support Greek opponents of Macedon in the First Macedonian War.
- 215–212 BCE: Roman forces under the Scipio brothers fought Carthaginian armies in Spain, preventing major reinforcements from reaching Hannibal.
- 215–214 BCE: After the death of Hiero II, his successor allied Syracuse with Carthage; his assassination allowed pro-Carthaginian leaders to gain control.
- 214–212 BCE: Rome besieged and captured Syracuse despite defenses developed by Archimedes, restoring Roman dominance in Sicily (and grain).
- 212 BCE: Hannibal captured Tarentum but failed to take its Roman-held citadel.
- 212–211 BCE: Rome besieged and recaptured Capua despite Hannibal's attempt to divert Roman forces by threatening Rome itself.
- 211 BCE: Both Scipio brothers were killed in separate defeats in Spain, temporarily reversing Roman gains.
- ~211–203 BCE: Rome gradually recovered Italian cities that had defected to Hannibal, weakening his position.
- 210 BCE: Scipio Africanus volunteered command of Roman forces in Spain.
- 209 BCE: Scipio captured New Carthage, securing a major Carthaginian base in Spain and gaining support from local communities.
- 208 BCE: Scipio defeated Hasdrubal (Hannibal's brother) at Baecula, but he escaped and marched toward Italy; Rome defeated him before he could reinforce Hannibal.
- 206 BCE: Scipio defeated Carthaginian forces at Ilipa, establishing Roman dominance in Spain.
- 205–204 BCE: Scipio prepared for an invasion of North Africa from Sicily and secured the support of the Numidian prince Masinissa (cavalry).
- 204–203 BCE: Scipio invaded North Africa and defeated Carthaginian and Numidian forces, forcing Carthage to recall Hannibal from Italy.
- 202 BCE: Scipio defeated Hannibal at the Battle of Zama, ending the Second Punic War.
- 201 BCE: Carthage accepted peace terms, surrendering its overseas possessions, paying an indemnity, and losing the right to wage war without Roman permission.

### Second Macedonian War

- 201–200 BCE: Pergamum and Rhodes sought Roman help against Philip V's expansion. Rome issued an ultimatum and began the war when Philip refused.
- 199 BCE: Roman forces advanced into Macedonia but struggled with mountainous terrain and Macedonian defensive positions.
- 198 BCE: Flamininus took command of Roman forces, gaining Greek allies by presenting the war as a struggle for Greek freedom.
- 197 BCE: Rome defeated Philip V at Cynoscephalae, demonstrating the superiority of maniple against the phalanx.
- 196 BCE: Flamininus proclaimed the freedom of Greek states, but Rome retained influence and expected cooperation and tribute.

### Roman–Seleucid War

- 195 BCE: Tensions grew between Rome and the Aetolian League, which felt excluded from the rewards and hoped to gain greater control over Greece.
- 192 BCE: Antiochus III of the Seleucid Empire invaded Greece with support from the Aetolian League, prompting Roman intervention.
- 191 BCE: Rome and Acahean League defeated Antiochus III at Thermopylae, forcing his withdrawal from Greece.
- 190 BCE: Roman forces (including Scipio Africanus) crossed into Asia Minor and decisively defeated Antiochus III at Magnesia.
- 190 BCE: Hannibal, serving Antiochus III, commanded a Seleucid fleet but was defeated by a Rhodian fleet.
- 188 BCE: Treaty of Apamea: Antiochus III has to surrender territories north-west of Taurus Mountains, pay an indemnity, and restrict his military power.

### Between the wars

- 183 BCE: Scipio Africanus died in retirement following political disputes and accusations against his family.
- 179 BCE: Philip V died, leaving the Macedonian throne to Perseus after a succession struggle involving his brother Demetrius (who was Romanized).

### Third Macedonian War

- 171 BCE: Rome declared war against Perseus, due to his suspected involvement in unrests in Illyria.
- 171–168 BCE: Roman campaigns in Greece were marked by military setbacks and widespread plundering, damaging relations with Greek communities.
- 168 BCE: Aemilius Paullus restored discipline and defeated Perseus at Pydna, ending the Macedonian monarchy.
- 167 BCE: Rome divided Macedonia into four separate republics, restricting their economic and political cooperation.
- 167 BCE: Roman forces devastated Epirus and enslaved large numbers of inhabitants as punishment for supporting Perseus.
- 167 BCE: Rome deported hundreds of Achaean political figures to Italy, including the historian Polybius who later wrote a history of Rome.

### Fourth Macedonian War and Achaean War

- 149–148 BCE: Andriscus attempted to restore the Macedonian monarchy but was defeated by Metellus; Macedonia became a province.
- 146 BCE: Rome defeated the Achaean League and destroyed Corinth, consolidating Roman dominance over Greece.

### Third Punic War

- 151 BCE: Carthage fought Numidia over territorial disputes, violating its peace treaty with Rome.
- 149 BCE: Rome declared war on Carthage and demanded that its inhabitants abandon the city and relocate inland.
- 149–147 BCE: Roman forces struggled to capture Carthage during a prolonged siege.
- 146 BCE: Scipio Aemilianus captured and destroyed Carthage after fierce street fighting, enslaving many survivors.
