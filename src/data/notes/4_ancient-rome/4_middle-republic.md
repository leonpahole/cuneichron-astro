# Middle Roman republic

Rome emerged as the dominant power in central Italy, while the political integration of plebeians (consulship + first plebeian censor and dictator) strengthened the Republic. Rome increasingly became involved in neighboring conflicts, with longer wars against stronger opponents and shifting from defending its territory toward deliberate expansion (land, taxation, and manpower).

Phalanx was replaced with maniple. The infantry was divided into smaller maneuverable units arranged in three lines. It was combined with light infantry (for beginning skirmish) and cavalry (on sides). Allies had to provide forces, especially cavalry. Soldiers fought with thrown spears before closing with swords. Focus was also on discipline (rewards and punishments), logistics, infrastrucutre (Appian way), fortified camps, and careful planning. The legion consisted of 10 maniples of 120 young, 10 maniples of 120 experienced, and 10 half strength maniples (60) of veterans + 1200 light infantry and 300 cavalry (4500 men in total).

## Timeline

### First Samnite War

- 343–341 BCE: Rome fought the First Samnite War after becoming involved in the conflict between Campania and the Samnites, semi-nomads from central Apennines (Samnium) who came down from the hills in 300s BC. `geo:{"type":"Polygon","coordinates":[[[14.0,41.0],[15.0,41.2],[14.8,42.0],[13.4,42.3],[13.1,41.5],[13.6,41.0],[14.0,41.0]]]}`
- 342 BCE: Plebeian reforms required at least one of the two consuls each year to be a plebeian and ten-year period before a person could hold the same office again. `geo:{"type":"Point","coordinates":[12.4964,41.9028]}`
- ~341 BCE: The First Samnite War ended with Rome having gained the upper hand but without decisively defeating the Samnites.
- ~341 BCE: A mutiny by Roman soldiers in Campania was peacefully resolved by Marcus Valerius Corvus, who secured amnesty for the soldiers. `geo:{"type":"Point","coordinates":[14.25,41.08]}`

### Latin War

- 340 BCE: The Latin War began after Latin League demanded full Roman citizenship and one of the consuls be from Latin communities.
- 340 BCE: Rome and the Latins fought at the foot of Mount Vesuvius in a closely matched battle, Rome victorious. `geo:{"type":"Point","coordinates":[14.4296,40.8214]}`
- ~340–338 BCE: Rome defeats Latin communities individually (Samnites assisting them), subduing Latium, abolishing Latin league, and granting different degrees of citizenship depending on their conduct. `geo:{"type":"Polygon","coordinates":[[[12.1,41.1],[13.2,41.2],[13.0,42.1],[11.9,42.1],[11.8,41.4],[12.1,41.1]]]}`
- ~338 BCE: Roman colonies were established on land taken from communities that had resisted Rome.

### Second Samnite War

- 327 BCE: The Second Samnite War began after Rome intervened in Naples at the request of its Greek nobility and expelled a Samnite garrison. `geo:{"type":"Point","coordinates":[14.25,40.85]}`
- ~321 BCE: Major Roman defeat at the Caudine Forks after the Samnites trapped their army in a mountain pass, Rome accepts harsh peace terms (according to sources, they didn't accept but could be propaganda). `geo:{"type":"Point","coordinates":[13.3,41.12]}`
- ~321–316 BCE: Fighting between Rome and the Samnites paused for ~five years.
- 315 BCE: Major Roman defeat at Lautulae; Campania and other allies threatened to defect. `geo:{"type":"Point","coordinates":[13.2,41.05]}`
- 312 BCE: Appius Claudius Caecus began construction of the Appian Way (Rome - Capua), improving movement of troops and supplies into south.
- 312 BCE: Appius Claudius Caecus began construction of an aqueduct to improve Rome's water supply.
- ~312 BCE: Rome replaced the phalanx with the more flexible maniple system due to struggles in the Samnite Wars.
- 311 BCE: The Etruscans entered the war against Rome, opening a second major front. `geo:{"type":"Polygon","coordinates":[[[10.0,43.2],[11.5,43.2],[12.2,42.5],[11.8,41.5],[11.0,41.3],[10.0,43.2]]]}`
- 308 BCE: The Etruscans made peace with Rome after their defeat at Lake Vadimo.
- 304 BCE: The Second Samnite War ended with Samnite defeat; Rome founded 13 colonies in Samnium and established garrisons.
- ~304 BCE: Roman citizenship was extended in varying forms to communities across Italy, allowing Rome to integrate defeated peoples while requiring them to provide soldiers.

### Third Samnite War

- 298 BCE: The Third Samnite War began after the Samnites resisted further Roman expansion and settlement in Samnium.
- ~297 BCE: The Samnites withdrew and joined forces with the Etruscans against Rome.
- 295 BCE: Umbrians and Gallic tribes from the Po Valley joined the anti-Roman coalition.
- 295 BCE: Rome defeated the coalition at the Battle of Sentinum. `geo:{"type":"Point","coordinates":[13.15,43.52]}`
- 293 BCE: Rome defeated the Samnites at Aquilonia. `geo:{"type":"Point","coordinates":[15.32,41.2]}`
- 290 BCE: The Third Samnite War ended, leaving Rome dominant across the Italian peninsula.

### Pyrrhic wars

- 282 BCE: Rome declared war on Tarentum (Magna Grecia) after a dispute with fleets.
- 280 BCE: Pyrrhus invaded Italy with an army including war elephants (Rome's first battle against them), defeating them at Heraclea. `geo:{"type":"Point","coordinates":[16.16,40.30]}`
- 279 BCE: Pyrrhus defeated Rome at the Battle of Asculum, but suffered heavy losses (“Pyrrhic victory”).
- 278 BCE: Pyrrhus left Italy for Sicily to drive the Carthaginians from the island.
- 278–276 BCE: Pyrrhus drove Carthaginian forces back but failing to capture their remaining stronghold.
- 275 BCE: Pyrrhus returned to Italy and was defeated at Beneventum, withdrawing to Epirus.
- 272 BCE: Rome captured Tarentum, completing its conquest of Magna Graecia. `geo:{"type":"Point","coordinates":[17.24,40.47]}`
