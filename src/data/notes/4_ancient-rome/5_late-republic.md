# Late Roman republic

Rome's Mediterranean conquests brought enormous wealth, slaves, and opportunities for political advancement, but these benefits were distributed unevenly. Aristocrats established personal patronage networks abroad and competed for lucrative military commands and provincial appointments.

In Italy, wealthy landowners increasingly consolidated agricultural estates, often employing slaves. Small farmers faced pressures, and some migrated to cities for work. As fewer citizens met the property qualifications for recruitment, they were gradually lowered.

Tensions between the ruling elite and poorer citizens intensified: disputes over land distribution, military service, and political representation increasingly challenged the Senate's authority. Politicians began appealing directly to popular assemblies, leading to split of populares and optimates.

Recruitment of poorer volunteers contributed to the gradual development of longer-serving armies whose soldiers increasingly depended on commanders for rewards and post-service settlement.

Individual generals were increasingly given more authority, reflecting the Republic's increasing reliance on powerful individual commanders.

### Tiberius Gracchus

- 168 BCE: Born into a prominent Roman family (mother [person]Cornelia[/person] was daughter of [person]Scipio Africanus[/person]).
- 146 BCE: Began his military career serving under [person]Scipio Aemilianus[/person] during the destruction of Carthage.
- 137 BCE: In Hispania negotiated a treaty to save a trapped Roman army; Senate rejected the agreement, damaging his political standing.
- 133 BCE: Elected tribune and proposed agrarian reforms to address land concentration: land cap and redistribution to poor.
- 133 BCE: Tribune [person]Marcus Octavius[/person] vetoed the proposal; Tiberius secured his removal, challenging the traditional tribune protections.
- 133 BCE: Tiberius bypassed the Senate and secured passage of his agrarian law through the popular assembly.
- 133 BCE: A three-member land commission began surveying and redistributing public land.
- 133 BCE: King [person]Attalus III[/person] of Pergamum died and bequeathed his kingdom to Rome; Tiberius proposed using the inheritance to finance his agrarian reforms.
- 133 BCE: Facing political prosecution after his term, Tiberius sought re-election, provoking accusations of personal rule.
- 133 BCE: [person]Tiberius Gracchus[/person] and many supporters were killed by a group of senators during an electoral assembly.

### Gaius Gracchus

- 154 BCE: Born into a prominent Roman family; his mother [person]Cornelia[/person] raised him.
- 133 BCE: Following the murder of Tiberius, Gaius inherited the political expectations and support networks.
- 126 BCE: Served as quaestor in Sardinia; repeated extensions of his commander's appointment kept him away from Rome.
- 124 BCE: Returned to Rome without authorization and won election as tribune.
- 123 BCE: Introduced a grain law to sell grain at a fixed, subsidized price, helping protect poor against high food prices.
- 123 BCE: Introduced legislation restricting capital punishment without popular authorization.
- 123 BCE: Revived his brother's agrarian reforms, restoring efforts for land redistribution.
- 123 BCE: Military reforms, including restrictions on recruiting under 17 and requiring the state to provide clothing.
- 123–122 BCE: Reformed the courts, transferring jury from senators to members of the equestrian order and weakening senatorial control over prosecutions.
- 123 BCE: Secured a second consecutive tribunate.
- 122 BCE: Proposed extending Roman citizenship to Latin communities and other Italian allies, but faced opposition from senators and population.
- 122 BCE: Promoted the establishment of new colonies, including in former Carthage, to reduce land pressure in Italy.
- 122 BCE: Introduced changes to provincial taxation.
- 122 BCE: Travelled to North Africa to oversee preparations for the colony; his political opponents gained influence during his absence.
- 122 BCE: Failed to secure election to a third tribunate.
- 121 BCE: Political tensions escalated after a supporter of Gaius killed an attendant of the consul Lucius Opimius during a confrontation.
- 121 BCE: The Senate issued the senatus consultum ultimum (emergency decree) urging the consuls to protect the Republic, which Opimius used to justify armed action against Gaius and his supporters.
- 121 BCE: [person]Gaius Gracchus[/person] and Marcus Fulvius Flaccus died during the violent suppression of their movement; many supporters were subsequently executed.
- 121 BCE: Some Gracchan reforms survived their creators, although the agrarian programme was gradually weakened and the proposal to extend citizenship to Italian allies failed.

### Marius

- 134–133 BCE: Served under [person]Scipio Aemilianus[/person] during the Numantine War, gaining military experience and recognition.
- 119 BCE: Elected tribune of the plebs; reforms restricting interference with voters.
- 115 BCE: Elected praetor, advancing his career despite his status as a novus homo.
- 115 BCE: [person]Marcus Licinius Crassus[/person] born into a wealthy Roman aristocratic family.
- ~114–113 BCE: Governed Further Spain, conducting military operations against local resistance.
- ~110 BCE: Married [person]Julia[/person], a member of the prominent Julii Caesares family.
- 109 BCE: Accompanied Metellus to Numidia as his senior lieutenant during the Jugurthine War.
- 109 BCE: The Cimbri defeated a Roman army commanded by Marcus Junius Silanus in southern Gaul.
- 107 BCE: Won consulship by criticizing Metellus's slow campaign against Jugurtha and promising a decisive victory.
- 107 BCE: Recruited volunteers without traditional property qualifications, opening military service more widely to landless citizens.
- 107 BCE: The Tigurini, a group associated with the Helvetii, defeated a Roman army under Lucius Cassius Longinus.
- 106 BCE: [person]Marius[/person]'s quaestor [person]Sulla[/person] negotiated with king of Mauretania to secure his betrayal.
- 106 BCE: [person]Pompey the Great[/person] born into a prominent Italian family with a strong military and political background.
- 105 BCE: Jugurtha surrendered to [person]Sulla[/person], ending the war and beginning the rivalry between [person]Marius[/person] and [person]Sulla[/person].
- 105 BCE: The Cimbri and their allies annihilated Roman armies commanded by Caepio and Gnaeus Mallius Maximus at Arausio due to disputes.
- 104–100 BCE: The Second Servile War (Sicily), due to disputes over the enslavement from Rome's allied communities; suppressed by Rome.
- ~104–103 BCE: The Cimbri moved into Hispania while the Teutones remained in Gaul, giving Rome time to rebuild its forces under [person]Marius[/person].
- ~104–100 BCE: Adopted the cohort as principal tactical unit, standardized equpiment, recruited poor, carrying their own equipment and intensifying training.
- 102 BCE: Defeated the Teutones and Ambrones at Aquae Sextiae, destroying a major invading force.
- 102 BCE: The Cimbri crossed the Alps into northern Italy, overcoming Roman attempts to block their advance.
- 101 BCE: With his consular colleague defeated the Cimbri at the Battle of Vercellae, ending the immediate threat.
- 100 BCE: Serving his sixth consulship, damaged his relationship with popular movement by suppressing political unrest.
- 100 BCE: Gaius Julius [person]Caesar[/person] born into the ancient patrician Julii family, which had lost much of its former political prominence.

### Social war

- ~125–91 BCE: Italian allies increasingly demanded Roman citizenship as they contributed to Rome's military campaigns without enjoying equal rights.
- 91 BCE: Tribune Drusus proposed extending citizenship to Italian allies, but he was assasinated, triggering the war.
- 91 BCE: Several Italian peoples, particularly Marsi and Samnites, rebelled and established an independent confederation with its capital at Corfinium.
- 90 BCE: Rome suffered several military defeats; Gaius [person]Marius[/person] returned to military command.
- 90–89 BCE: Rome passed laws extending citizenship to loyal allies and rebels willing to accept Roman authority, gradually undermining the rebellion.
- 89 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] achieved major victories against the southern rebels, while Pompeius Strabo captured the important stronghold of Asculum.
- ~89–87 BCE: The Social War ended with most Italian communities gaining Roman citizenship.
