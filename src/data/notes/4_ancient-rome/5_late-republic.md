# Late Roman republic

Rome's Mediterranean conquests brought wealth, slaves, and opportunities for political advancement, but these benefits were distributed unevenly. Aristocrats established personal patronage networks abroad and competed for lucrative military commands and provincial appointments.

In Italy, wealthy landowners consolidated agricultural estates, often employing slaves. Small farmers faced pressures, and some migrated to cities for work. As fewer citizens met the property qualifications for recruitment, they were gradually lowered. Armies became longer-serving and soldiers depended on commanders for rewards and post-service settlement. Individual generals were given more authority.

Tensions between the ruling elite and poorer citizens intensified. Because aristocrats died during the Second Punic War, more _novus homo_ entered politics, and they had no interest in upkeeping existing agreements between high-class. They became populist, leading to split of populares and optimates.

## Timeline

### [person]Tiberius Gracchus[/person]

- 168 BCE: Born into a prominent Roman family (mother [person:cornelia-gracchi]Cornelia[/person] was daughter of [person]Scipio Africanus[/person]).
- 146 BCE: Served under [person]Scipio Aemilianus[/person] during the destruction of Carthage.
- 137 BCE: In Hispania negotiated a treaty to save a trapped army; Senate rejected it, damaging his political standing and turning him to populism.
- 133 BCE: Elected tribune and proposed land cap and redistribution; a Senate-backed tribune vetoed the proposal and Tiberius removed him, challenging traditional tribune protections. He then bypassed Senate to pass the law through popular assembly.
- 133 BCE: A three-member commission began surveying and redistributing public land. Tiberius proposed using land King [person]Attalus III[/person] of Pergamum willed to Rome to finance the reforms.
- 133 BCE: Sought re-election to avoid political prosecution after his term, which provoked accusations of personal rule. Killed along with many supporters by a group of senators during an electoral assembly.

### [person]Gaius Gracchus[/person]

- 154-133 BCE: Early life, [person:cornelia-gracchi]Cornelia[/person] raised him, inherited political networks and expectations from his brother.
- 126 BCE: Served as quaestor in Sardinia; repeated extensions of his commander's appointment kept him away from Rome.
- 124 BCE: Returned to Rome without authorization and won election as tribune.
- 123 BCE: Reforms: selling grain at a subsidized price to address rising prices, restricting capital punishment without popular authorization, revived agrarian reforms, military (restrictions on recruiting under 17 and state-provided clothing), courts (jury from senators to equestrians).
- 122 BCE: Second tribunate: proposed extending Roman citizenship to Italian allies (not passed; faced opposition from senators and population), establishment of new Colonies (including in former Carthage) to reduce land pressure, changes to provincial taxation.
- 122 BCE: Travelled to Carthage to oversee colony preparations; his opponents gained influence during his absence. Failed to secure third tribunate.
- 121 BCE: Tensions escalated after his supporter killed an attendant of a consul; Senate issued an emergency decree urging the consuls to protect the Republic, giving consults justification to use armed action. Gaius and many supporters died during the violent suppression of his movement.

### [person]Marius[/person]

- 157 BCE: Born near Arpinum in Latium, into a locally prominent family.
- 134–133 BCE: Served under [person]Scipio Aemilianus[/person] during the Numantine War, gaining experience and recognition.
- 119 BCE: Elected tribune; reforms restricting interference with voters.
- 115 BCE: Elected praetor, advancing his career despite his status as a novus homo.
- 115 BCE: [person]Crassus[/person] born into a wealthy Roman aristocratic family.
- ~114–113 BCE: Governed Further Spain, conducting military operations against local resistance.
- ~110 BCE: Married [person]Julia[/person], a member of the prominent Julii Caesares family.
- 109 BCE: Accompanied Metellus to Numidia during the Jugurthine War and won consulship by criticizing the slow campaign, promising a quick victory.
- 107 BCE: Recruited volunteers without traditional property qualifications.
- 106 BCE: His quaestor [person]Sulla[/person] negotiated with king of Mauretania to secure betraying Jugurtha.
- 105 BCE: Jugurtha surrendered to [person]Sulla[/person], ending the war and beginning the rivalry between [person]Marius[/person] and [person]Sulla[/person].
- 109-107 BCE: The Cimbri and Tigurini defeated Roman armies near southern Gaul multiple times.
- 106 BCE: [person]Pompey[/person] born into a prominent Italian family with a strong military and political background.
- 105 BCE: The Cimbri and their allies annihilated Roman armies at Arausio, partly due to disputes between two consults.
- ~104–103 BCE: The Cimbri moved into Hispania while the Teutones remained in Gaul, giving Rome time to rebuild its forces under [person]Marius[/person].
- ~104–100 BCE: Adopted cohort as main tactical unit, standardized equpiment, recruited poor, carrying their own equipment and intensified training.
- 102 BCE: Defeated the Teutones and Ambrones at Aquae Sextiae.
- 101 BCE: Defeated the Cimbri who crossed the Alps at the Battle of Vercellae, ending the immediate threat.
- 100 BCE: Serving his sixth consulship, damaged his relationship with popular movement by suppressing political unrest.

### Social war

- ~125–91 BCE: Italian allies demanded citizenship as they contributed to Rome's military campaigns without enjoying equal rights.
- 91 BCE: A tribune proposed extending citizenship to Italian allies, but was assasinated, triggering several Italian peoples (including Samnites) to rebel and establish an independent confederation with capital at Corfinium.
- 90 BCE: Rome suffered several military defeats; [person]Marius[/person] returned to military command.
- 90–89 BCE: Rome passed laws extending citizenship to loyal allies and rebels willing to accept Roman authority, gradually undermining the rebellion.
- 89 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] achieved major victories against the southern rebels, while Pompeius Strabo captured the stronghold of Asculum.
- ~89–87 BCE: The war ended with most Italian communities gaining Roman citizenship.

### [person]Sulla[/person] - First Mithridatic War

- 88 BCE: [person]Mithridates VI[/person] of Pontus invaded Roman-controlled territories in Asia Minor and orchestrated the Asiatic Vespers. A struggle over command of the war led [person]Sulla[/person] to march his army on Rome, [person]Marius[/person] fled to exile.
- 87 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] departed to fight [person]Mithridates VI[/person], while political conflict resumed between the consuls Cinna and Gnaeus Octavius. Cinna was expelled and allied with returning [person]Marius[/person]; their forces captured the city and carried out violent reprisals.
- 86 BCE: [person]Marius[/person] began his seventh consulship alongside Cinna but died shortly afterward, leaving Cinna as the dominant figure.
- 86 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] captured and sacked Athens after a prolonged siege, then defeated [person]Mithridates VI[/person] at Chaeronea and Orchomenus, securing control of mainland Greece.
- 86–85 BCE: Rome dispatched a rival army to the east; after a mutiny it fought [person]Mithridates VI[/person] independently.
- 85 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] concluded the Peace of Dardanus with [person]Mithridates VI[/person], requiring him to abandon his conquests while retaining kingdom of Pontus.
- 85–84 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] imposed heavy financial penalties on cities in Roman Asia.

### [person]Sulla[/person] the dictator

- 84 BCE: Cinna killed during a military mutiny while preparing forces to confront [person]Sulla[/person].
- 83 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] returned to Italy to overthrow the Marian government, many commanders defected to his side.
- 83–82 BCE: [person]Pompey[/person], [person]Crassus[/person], and Metellus Pius supported [person]Sulla[/person], helping him defeat the Marian forces.
- 82 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] defeated the son of Marius at Sacriportus and besieged him at Praeneste, while his allies defeated other Marian armies across Italy.
- 82 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] defeated a Samnite-led army at the Battle of the Colline Gate, securing control of the capital; [person]Crassus[/person] playing a decisive part in victory.
- 82 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] was appointed dictator with extraordinary powers and no fixed term limit.
- 82–81 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] introduced the proscriptions: lists of political enemies, property was confiscated, enriching his supporters.
- 82–81 BCE: [person]Pompey[/person] defeated remaining Marian forces in Sicily and Africa, consolidating [person]Sulla[/person]'s control.
- 82–78 BCE: [person]Crassus[/person] accumulated enormous wealth by purchasing confiscated property and investing in land, mining, and slaves.
- 81-80 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] expanded the Senate, restricted tribunal power, reorganized the cursus honorum, transferred jury control to senators and settled his veterans on confiscated Italian land.
- 81 BCE: [person]Pompey[/person] celebrated a triumph for his victories in Africa despite never having held a magistracy, bypassing traditions.
- 81 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] proscribed [person]Caesar[/person] as he refused to divorce daughter of Cinna [person:cornelia-cinnae]Cornelia[/person], briefly going into hiding.
- ~80–79 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] voluntarily resigned his dictatorship and returned to ordinary political life, eventually retiring from public office.
- 78 BCE: [person]Sulla[/person] died.
- 78–77 BCE: A consul attempted to challenge established order but was defeated by forces loyal to the Senate, including [person]Pompey[/person].
- 77 BCE: [person]Pompey[/person] received proconsular command in Hispania despite lacking qualifications, campaigning against the Marian commander [person]Sertorius[/person].
- 77–72 BCE: [person]Sertorius[/person] successfully resisted Roman armies in Hispania.
- 72 BCE: [person]Sertorius[/person] assassinated, [person]Pompey[/person] ends major resistance in Hispania.

### Servile Wars

- 135–132 BCE: The First Servile War (Sicily); slaves rebelled against harsh treatment by wealthy landowners; suppressed by Rome.
- 104–100 BCE: The Second Servile War (Sicily), due to disputes over the enslavement from Rome's allied communities; suppressed by Rome.
- 73 BCE: ~70 gladiators, led by [person]Spartacus[/person] and [person]Crixus[/person], escaped from a gladiatorial school in Capua, beginning the Third Servile War. They established a base near Vesuvius, attracted slaves and defeated several Roman forces sent to suppress them.
- 72 BCE: The rebel army divided, with [person]Crixus[/person] remaining in southern Italy while [person]Spartacus[/person] marched north. [person]Crixus[/person] was defeated and killed by Rome. [person]Spartacus[/person] defeated Roman consular armies but then turned south for uncertain reasons.
- 72–71 BCE: [person]Crassus[/person] received command against [person]Spartacus[/person] and imposed strict military discipline.
- 71 BCE: [person]Spartacus[/person] attempted to cross into Sicily but was abandoned by pirates. [person]Crassus[/person] trapped his army and defeated them. [person]Pompey[/person] intercepted and destroyed a separate group of fleeing rebels, claiming he won the war.
- 71 BCE: [person]Crassus[/person] ordered ~6,000 captured rebels crucified along the Appian Way as a warning against future uprisings.
- 70 BCE: [person]Crassus[/person] and [person]Pompey[/person] became consuls together despite their rivalry.

### Third Mithridatic War and [person]Pompey[/person]'s conquests

- 74 BCE: King [person]Nicomedes IV[/person] of Bithynia died and bequeathed his kingdom to Rome, provoking conflict with [person]Mithridates VI[/person] of Pontus.
- 73–72 BCE: Mithridates invaded Bithynia. Roman commander [person]Lucullus[/person] defeated his forces and invaded Pontus.
- 71–70 BCE: [person]Lucullus[/person] conquered much of Pontus, forcing Mithridates to flee to Armenia, where he sought protection from son-in-law King [person]Tigranes II[/person].
- 69 BCE: [person]Lucullus[/person] invaded Armenia and defeated [person]Tigranes II[/person] at the Battle of Tigranocerta.
- 68–67 BCE: [person]Lucullus[/person]'s campaigns stalled amid difficult conditions, weakening his political support in Rome.
- 67 BCE: The Lex Gabinia granted [person]Pompey[/person] authority across the Mediterranean to combat piracy; his naval campaign defeated and resettled pirates.
- 66 BCE: The Lex Manilia transferred command of the war against Mithridates to [person]Pompey[/person].
- 66 BCE: [person]Pompey[/person] defeated Mithridates in Pontus; [person]Tigranes II[/person] submitted to Rome, Armenia became a client kingdom.
- 66–65 BCE: [person]Pompey[/person] campaigned in the Caucasus against local kingdoms, extending Roman military influence beyond Anatolia.
- 64 BCE: [person]Pompey[/person] annexed remaining Seleucid territories in Syria.
- 63 BCE: [person]Pompey[/person] intervened in dynastic conflict in Judea, captured Jerusalem, and established Roman dominance over the kingdom.
- 63 BCE: [person]Mithridates VI[/person] died after losing his remaining political and military support.
- 61 BCE: [person]Pompey[/person] returned to Rome and celebrated a triumph, having expanded Roman influence and doubled tax revenues.

### [person]Caesar[/person] - Early career

- 100 BCE: Born into the ancient patrician Julii family, which had lost much of its former political prominence.
- ~87–82 BCE: Married [person:cornelia-cinnae]Cornelia[/person], daughter of Cinna, and was nominated as Flamen Dialis (priest of Jupiter).
- 81 BCE: Went into hiding after [person]Sulla[/person] stripped his priesthood and proscribed him as he refused to divorce [person:cornelia-cinnae]Cornelia[/person].
- 80 BCE: Served in army in Asia Minor and received the corona civica for bravery during the siege of Mytilene.
- ~75 BCE: Captured by pirates while travelling to Rhodes, later capturing and executing them.
- 69 BCE: Served as quaestor in Hispania Ulterior. At his aunt [person]Julia[/person]'s funeral, he publicly displayed images of her husband [person]Marius[/person], helping rehabilitiate his image.
- 65 BCE: As aedile, financed lavish public games and building projects, gaining popularity while accumulating debts.
- 63 BCE: Elected Pontifex Maximus, Rome's chief priest.
- 62 BCE: Served as praetor, aligning himself with [person]Pompey[/person]'s while maintaining financial ties with [person]Crassus[/person].

### Caitiline conspiracy

- ~80s BCE: [person]Cicero[/person] established himself as a prominent lawyer and orator, combining Roman legal practice with Greek philosophy and rhetoric.
- 64 BCE: [person]Cicero[/person] defeated [person]Catiline[/person] in the consular elections, benefiting from senatorial opposition to [person]Catiline[/person]'s proposals for debt relief.
- 63 BCE: [person]Catiline[/person] lost another consular election and organized a conspiracy against the government, attracting indebted aristocrats, disaffected veterans, and other establishment opponents. [person]Cicero[/person] exposed the conspiracy through a series of speeches to the Senate; [person]Catiline[/person] fled Rome and joined an armed rebellion in Etruria. Gallic envoys revealed evidence of conspirators, allowing [person]Cicero[/person] to arrest several participants and execute some.
- 62 BCE: Roman forces defeated [person]Catiline[/person]'s rebel army at the Battle of Pistoria, killing [person]Catiline[/person] and ending the uprising.

### [person]Caesar[/person] - Rise

- 61 BCE: Conducted successful campaigns in western Iberia as governor of Hispania Ulterior, acquiring wealth and military prestige.
- 60 BCE: Abandoned the opportunity to celebrate a triumph so that he could stand for the consulship, overcoming opposition from [person]Cato the Younger[/person].
- 60 BCE: Won election as consul and entered an informal political alliance with [person]Pompey[/person] and [person]Crassus[/person] (First Triumvirate).
- 59 BCE: Became consul alongside Bibulus and despite his opposition, passed agrarian reform for land distribution (settling [person]Pompey[/person]'s veterans), approval of [person]Pompey[/person]'s eastern conquests and stricter regulations against provincial corruption.
- 59 BCE: [person]Pompey[/person] married [person]Caesar[/person]'s daughter [person]Julia[/person], strengthening their alliance.
- 59 BCE: Retaliated against [person]Cicero[/person]'s opposition by allowing [person]Clodius[/person] into a plebeian family, enabling him to seek the tribunate.
- 59 BCE: Secured 5-year provincial of Illyricum, Cisalpine and Transalpine Gaul; oferring military opportunities and protection from prosecution.

### [person]Caesar[/person] - The Gallic wars

- 58 BCE: The Helvetii (Celts) began migrating westward into Gaul, creating tensions with neighboring tribes and Roman interests, [person]Caesar[/person] refused their passage and costructeed fortifications along the Rhône. After they took an alternate path through Gaul, his Gallic allies asked for support and [person]Caesar[/person] pursued and defeated them (Arar, Bibracte); survivors had to return to their homeland, serving as a buffer against Germans.
- 58 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person] confronted [person]Ariovistus[/person], a Germanic ruler that was expanding into Gaul. After negotiations failed, he defeated him at Vosges, forcing the survivors to retreat across the Rhine. Rather than withdrawing, [person]Caesar[/person] stationed his legions in winter quarters within independent Gaul.
- 57-56 BCE: Defeated Belgic tribes in northern Gaul, and suppressed revolt by Veneti (maritime people of Brittany).
- 56 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person], [person]Pompey[/person], and [person]Crassus[/person] renewed their political alliance at the Conference of Luca.
- 55 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person] constructed a wooden bridge across the Rhine and led a brief expedition into Germania to send a message.
- 55 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person]'s first expedition to Britain was forced to withdraw due to local resistance, logistics and storm damage.
- 54 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person] returned to Britain with a larger army, defeating rebels and securing agreements with several British rulers before withdrawing.
- 54 BCE: [person]Julia[/person] died in childbirth, weakening his relationship with [person]Pompey[/person].
- 54–53 BCE: Gallic rebels destroyed a Roman force and besieged another; [person]Caesar[/person] relieved them and launched campaigns against rebels, crossing the Rhine again to deter Germanic intervention.
- 52 BCE: [person]Vercingetorix[/person] united several Gallic tribes in a major revolt, adopting scorched-earth tactics. [person]Caesar[/person] captured Avaricum after a prolonged siege, but suffered a significant defeat at Gergovia.
- 52 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person] besieged [person]Vercingetorix[/person] at Alesia, constructing two rings of fortifications; [person]Vercingetorix[/person] surrendered.

### Set up to civil war

- 58 BCE: Tribune [person]Clodius[/person] forced [person]Cicero[/person] into exile, introduced free grain distributions and annexed Cyprus, arranging for [person]Cato the Younger[/person] to oversee it, removing him from Rome.
- 57 BCE: [person]Cicero[/person] returned from exile, rival gangs associated with [person]Clodius[/person] and Milo increasingly used violence.
- 55 BCE: Co-consuls [person]Pompey[/person] and [person]Crassus[/person] extended [person]Caesar[/person]'s command in Gaul and secured commands of Hispania ([person]Pompey[/person]) and Syria ([person]Crassus[/person]).
- 54 BCE: [person]Crassus[/person] assembled a large army in Syria to invade the Parthian Empire.
- 53 BCE: Battle of Carrhae - Parthian cavalry defeated [person]Crassus[/person]; [person]Crassus[/person] and his son Publius were killed.
- 53 BCE: [person]Crassus[/person]'s and [person]Julia[/person]'s death ended the First Triumvirate and politically separated [person]Caesar[/person] and [person]Pompey[/person].
- 52 BCE: [person]Clodius[/person] was killed during a confrontation, triggering riots; his supporters burned the Senate house during his funeral.
- 52 BCE: Senate appointed [person]Pompey[/person] as sole consul to restore order.
- ~52–50 BCE: [person]Pompey[/person] aligned himself with [person]Caesar[/person]'s opponents who disputed over his command, potential prosecution, and right to consulship.
- 50 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person] proposed relinquishing his military command if [person]Pompey[/person] did likewise, but negotiations failed.

### Civil war

- January 49 BCE: Tribunes including [person]Mark Antony[/person] opposed the senate and fled to [person]Caesar[/person]'s camp.
- 10 January 49 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person] crossed the Rubicon with Legio XIII.
- January–March 49 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person] encountered limited resistance and showed clemency. [person]Pompey[/person] and many senators withdrew south.
- March 49 BCE: [person]Pompey[/person] evacuated from Brundisium to Greece, escaping [person]Caesar[/person]'s siege and blockade.
- April 49 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person] entered Rome and seized funds from the treasury to finance his army, despite tribune opposition.
- Summer 49 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person] forced surrender of [person]Pompey[/person]'s legions in Hispania and some joined him istead.
- December 49 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person] briefly assumed the dictatorship to organize elections that secured his consulship.
- January 48 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person] crossed the Adriatic, surprising [person]Pompey[/person]'s forces. However, his remaining troops were blocked from immediately following.
- Spring 48 BCE: [person]Mark Antony[/person] transported remaining reinforcements across the Adriatic despite the blockade.
- ~July 48 BCE: Battle of Dyrrhachium - [person]Pompey[/person] broke through [person]Caesar[/person]'s fortifications and defeated his forces, but failed to pursue out of fear.
- 9 August 48 BCE: Battle of Pharsalus - [person]Caesar[/person] decisively defeated [person]Pompey[/person]'s numerically superior army.
- August–September 48 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person] pardoned several defeated opponents, including Brutus, continuing his policy of clemency.
- 28 September 48 BCE: [person]Pompey[/person] was assassinated upon arriving in Egypt by agents of [person]Ptolemy XIII[/person]'s government, who hoped to gain [person]Caesar[/person]'s support; [person]Caesar[/person] was disgusted.
- October 48 BCE: [person]Caesar[/person] arrived in Alexandria and intervened in the dynastic struggle between [person]Cleopatra VII[/person] and her brother [person]Ptolemy XIII[/person].
- 48–47 BCE: Alexandrian War - [person]Ptolemy XIII[/person]'s supporters fought [person]Caesar[/person] in Alexandria, leading to urban fighting and naval engagements.
- ~March 47 BCE: Battle of the Nile - [person]Caesar[/person] defeated [person]Ptolemy XIII[/person] and, rather than annexing Egypt, restored [person]Cleopatra VII[/person] under Roman protection.
- 47 BCE: Caesar defeated Pharnaces II of Pontus at Zela, ending his rebellion (veni, vidi, vici).
- 47 BCE: Caesar returned to Rome and removed Mark Antony from control of the city following unrest and his controversial behavior.
- 47 BCE: Caesar's veteran legions mutinied over unpaid rewards and delayed discharge. He persuaded them to resume service by promising eventual retirement.
- 6 April 46 BCE: Battle of Thapsus - Caesar decisively defeated remaining senatorial forces in North Africa. Cato the Younger committed suicide at Utica.
- 46 BCE: Caesar celebrated four triumphs in Rome (Gaul, Egypt, Pontus, and Africa). Vercingetorix was executed during the celebrations.
- ~46–44 BCE: Caesar's reforms: reducing the number of citizens receiving free grain, establishing overseas colonies for veterans and poorer citizens, extending citizenship to selected provincial communities, expanding the senate and admitting provincials into it, public works and urban improvements, and Julian calendar (365 days + leap year every 4 years).
